The Streaming Process Explained

Once the camera setup is in place, the streaming process begins. This process involves encoding the video feed into a suitable format for transmission over the internet. Advanced encoding solutions, like H.264 or H.265, compress the video to ensure it can be streamed without significant loss in quality. This is vital, as players expect smooth and uninterrupted gameplay. Any lag or buffering can detract from the overall player experience.

The live feed is then transmitted from the casino to the players' devices through various streaming protocols, such as RTMP (Real-Time Messaging Protocol) and WebRTC (Web Real-Time Communication). These protocols facilitate low-latency streaming, allowing for near-instant data transfer, which is crucial for maintaining real-time interaction between players and the dealer. Additionally, the use of Content Delivery Networks (CDNs) helps distribute the video feed globally, ensuring players from different regions can access the games without delays.

Furthermore, live casinos utilize adaptive bitrate streaming, which adjusts the quality of video feeds based on a player's internet connection speed. This technology ensures that players receive the best possible viewing experience without interruptions. If a player's connection is slower, the system automatically lowers the quality of the stream to prevent buffering, while those with faster connections can enjoy the highest-quality video. Such sophistication in the streaming process reflects the commitment of live casinos to deliver an optimal gaming experience to all players, regardless of their equipment or connection quality.
